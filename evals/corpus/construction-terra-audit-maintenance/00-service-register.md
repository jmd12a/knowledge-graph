# Service registry
This controlled register establishes software identities, not runtime calls. Business ownership does not imply request direction. Fleet Dispatch and Asset Health are display names shared by several deployments. Dispatch is an ambiguous shorthand for either production fleet service.

The staging tenant holds synthetic records and uses separate credentials; its real-sounding depot names are exercise labels.

## Bay handover

The morning controller checks the physical bay against the work board before releasing an allocation. A late vehicle keeps its provisional place only until the next inspection interval; the controller writes the reason on the handover sheet. The duty lead checks the entry at handover and asks the responsible person to correct an incomplete record. The shift pack is kept available for review.

## Key custody

Keys move from the transport supervisor to the technician using a numbered tag. A missing signature causes the supervisor to hold the vehicle and reconcile the cabinet, rather than assigning the key by memory. Any discrepancy is assigned a named owner and remains on the local exception list until it is resolved. The supervisor notes the time and next check.

## Isolation tags

An isolation tag states who applied the lock and which energy sources were checked. A second technician verifies the tag when a job spans two shifts, and the old tag remains attached until the replacement is accepted. The depot retains the signed sheet so a reviewer can follow the decision and its timing. Copies are checked for legibility before originals are filed.

## Defect triage

The duty engineer distinguishes a safety hold from a service recommendation. A recommendation can wait for a scheduled bay; a safety hold prevents release until a qualified person records the disposition. The shift lead records the constraint and agrees the next action with the responsible person. A change of shift does not close an outstanding action.

## Parts counter

The counter reserves a kit against the work order, then checks quantities when the technician collects it. Substitution requires the engineer to confirm compatibility and the removed part is labelled for return. This instruction describes a physical depot process. Its evidence is the signed local record and the people who completed the check.
